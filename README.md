# Seeke

## Art exhibitions on now.

[Seeke](https://seeke.art) is a mobile-first platform for discovering current art exhibitions and art opportunities. It helps people find what is happening nearby without having to piece together fragmented information across gallery sites, newsletters, and social feeds.

> This repository is a product showcase, not the production application or its internal infrastructure.

## The problem

Finding a worthwhile exhibition should be easy. In practice, current listings are scattered, inconsistently formatted, quickly outdated, and often difficult to browse on a phone. Seeke turns that fragmented landscape into a focused, current discovery experience.

## My role

I am Seeke's solo founder. I own the product from idea through release: product strategy, interaction and visual design, brand identity, frontend/product implementation, data architecture, editorial standards, automation workflows, and deployment.

I build with AI-assisted development tools as part of an iterative product process. AI helps me prototype, implement, and investigate quickly; product direction, design judgment, system decisions, and final review are mine.

## Product principles

- Put what is on now ahead of endless browsing.
- Make cultural discovery feel considered, not cluttered.
- Treat data quality and editorial judgment as part of the product experience.
- Design for the small screen first, where most casual discovery happens.

## Stack

- **Product application:** Next.js
- **Data and backend services:** Supabase
- **Deployment:** Vercel
- **Operations:** APIs, scheduled automation, and editorial/data-quality workflows
- **Design:** product design and visual systems created in Adobe tools

Third-party platforms provide infrastructure and AI capabilities; I design, assemble, operate, and iterate on the product and its workflows.

## How it works

At a high level, Seeke combines a customer-facing discovery experience with a carefully maintained exhibition dataset:

1. Source and update exhibition information through APIs, automation, and editorial review.
2. Normalize dates, venues, locations, and descriptive information so listings are comparable and current.
3. Serve a fast, mobile-first experience for exploring exhibitions and opportunities.
4. Use ongoing data-quality checks to keep the live catalog useful as listings change.

The production codebase, credentials, internal schema, and operational details remain private.

## Product problems I have worked through

- Designing a clear discovery experience for time-sensitive cultural information.
- Building a data workflow that balances automation with editorial quality control.
- Normalizing incomplete, inconsistent source data into useful listings.
- Creating a visual identity that makes an information-dense product feel calm and editorial.
- Iterating on a live product across product design, implementation, operations, and deployment.
